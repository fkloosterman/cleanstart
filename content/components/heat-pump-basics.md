## Moving heat, not making it

A furnace burns fuel to create heat. A heat pump instead **moves** heat from
one place to another using a refrigerant cycle — the same physics as a
refrigerator, run in whichever direction you need. In winter it pulls heat from
the outdoor air (even cold air holds heat) and delivers it inside; in summer it
runs in reverse and moves heat out, cooling the home.

Because it moves heat rather than generating it, a heat pump can deliver several
units of heat for each unit of electricity — which is why one efficient system
can replace both a furnace and an air conditioner.
